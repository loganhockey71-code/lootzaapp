Selling a digital product for the first time is mostly friction-avoidance: the fewer steps between "I have a file" and "someone can buy it," the more likely you actually finish. Here's what the process looks like in practice.

## 1. Pick the right category

Buyers browse by category — [Gaming](/categories/gaming), [Graphics](/categories/graphics), [Social](/categories/social), [Web](/categories/web), [Creator](/categories/creator), [AI](/categories/ai) — before they ever search by name. A product filed under the wrong category is invisible to the people actually looking for it, even if the listing itself is great. Pick the category your buyer would click first, not the one that technically fits best.

## 2. Write a title and tagline that says what it actually is

"Ultimate Mega Pack Vol. 3" tells a browsing buyer nothing. "100+ futuristic UI components" tells them exactly what they're getting in five words. Specificity outperforms hype — buyers skim, they don't read.

## 3. Attach the real file and a cover image

The product file itself is uploaded privately — buyers never get direct access to it before purchase; it's only released after payment succeeds. A cover image helps, but isn't required — Lootza generates a themed cover automatically if you skip it. Still, a real screenshot of the actual product converts better than a generic placeholder.

## 4. Describe what's included, specifically

List the actual contents — file counts, formats, what's in the box — rather than adjectives. "40 editable screens, .fig and .sketch formats, dark and light themes" tells a buyer exactly what they're paying for. Vague descriptions create refund requests; specific ones prevent them.

## 5. Price it by looking at comparable listings

Check what similar products in your category are actually charging before picking a number — see [the best digital products to sell](/blog/best-digital-products-to-sell) for how pricing tends to shake out by category. You can also set an original/discounted price pair to show a percentage off, which is a real conversion lever, not just decoration.

## 6. Set up payouts before your first paid listing goes live

If you're selling a paid product, Lootza requires a connected Stripe payout account to be set up before that listing can publish — this isn't a bureaucratic hurdle, it's what guarantees you actually get paid the moment a sale happens rather than sales piling up with no way to collect them. It's a one-time setup, done from your seller dashboard, and takes a couple of minutes. Free products skip this entirely.

## 7. Publish, then let discovery do its job

Once live, your listing shows up in category pages, search, and — depending on engagement — [Discover's](/discover) Trending and New Drops sections. Unlike a standalone storefront link you have to promote entirely yourself, a marketplace listing can pick up buyers who were never looking for you specifically.
