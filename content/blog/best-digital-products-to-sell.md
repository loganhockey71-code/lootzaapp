Digital products are one of the few businesses where you build something once and sell it indefinitely — no inventory, no shipping, no per-unit cost. That's also why the space is crowded. The products that actually sell aren't just "good," they solve a specific, recurring problem for a specific buyer.

Here are the categories that consistently perform well, and why.

## 1. UI kits and design systems

Designers and indie developers constantly need a starting point that doesn't look like a template. A well-organized [graphics category](/categories/graphics) listing — clean layers, a real style guide, both light and dark variants — saves a buyer days of work. That's the pitch: not "pretty," but "hours saved."

Lootza's [Neon Cyberpunk UI Kit](/product/neon-cyberpunk-ui-kit) is a good example of the format that works: a clear aesthetic, a large component count, and explicit compatibility (Figma, Sketch, Adobe XD) stated up front.

## 2. Notion templates and creator systems

Notion templates sell because they package a *workflow*, not just a document. Buyers aren't paying for a database — they're paying to skip the hour they'd otherwise spend building one from scratch. The best-performing templates are narrow and specific: a content calendar for creators, a client tracker for freelancers, a brand-deal CRM — not a vague "all-in-one life OS."

See the [Creator category](/categories/creator) for examples like the [Notion Creator OS Template](/product/notion-creator-os-template), which packages content planning, brand deals, and finances into one system rather than trying to be everything at once.

## 3. AI prompt packs and toolkits

This category is newer, moves fast, and rewards specificity. A generic "500 ChatGPT prompts" pack undersells; a prompt vault organized by actual use case (copywriting, coding, image generation) with tested, working prompts sells because it removes trial and error. Browse the [AI category](/categories/ai) for the current shape of what's working, including the [AI Prompt Vault](/product/ai-prompt-vault).

## 4. Web templates and landing kits

Every solo founder and freelancer needs a starting point for a site that doesn't look like a starting point. Templates that ship with real copy structure (not just "lorem ipsum" placeholders) and a working component library — see the [Web category](/categories/web) — tend to outsell purely visual mockups, because they save implementation time, not just design time.

## Pricing: don't guess, look at comparables

New sellers consistently underprice. Look at what similar products in your category are charging on [Discover](/discover) before setting a number — a UI kit with 100+ components competing against $12 packs is leaving money on the table if it's priced the same.
