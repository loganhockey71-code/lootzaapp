Gumroad and similar tools popularized the simple digital storefront: upload a file, get a checkout link, share it yourself. That model still works well if you already have an audience to send traffic to. It works less well if your real problem isn't *checkout* — it's *discovery*: getting found by buyers who weren't already looking for you specifically.

That's the actual question worth asking before picking a platform: **do you need a place to sell to people you already reach, or a place that can also help people find you?**

## Storefront tools vs. marketplaces

A pure storefront tool gives you a page and a payment link. It doesn't put your product in front of anyone who wasn't already coming to you. That's a reasonable trade if you have an audience elsewhere (a newsletter, a following, a community) and just need the transaction handled.

A marketplace works differently: your product sits alongside other products in the same category, gets discovered through search, browsing, and trending sections, and can pick up buyers who've never heard of you before. The trade-off is usually a platform fee in exchange for that discovery — which only makes sense if the discovery is real.

## What to actually check when comparing platforms

- **Is there real search and category browsing**, or is it just a hosted page with no path for a stranger to find it?
- **What's the actual fee structure**, and what do you get for it? A platform taking a cut with zero discovery traffic is the worst of both worlds.
- **Do you keep ownership of pricing and file delivery**, or are you locked into a specific format?
- **Is there a path to build a reputation** — reviews, follower counts, seller profiles — that compounds over time instead of resetting with every new product?
