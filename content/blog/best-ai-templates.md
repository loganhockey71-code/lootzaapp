The market for AI templates got noisy fast. Anyone can screenshot 50 ChatGPT prompts and sell a PDF. The packs actually worth paying for do something specific: they save you from the blank-page problem and the trial-and-error of figuring out what actually works.

Here's what to look for, and what a good one looks like in practice.

## Structure beats volume

"2,000 prompts" sounds impressive until you realize most prompt packs are dumping quantity to look substantial. A pack organized by real use case — content, code, brand voice, image generation — that you can actually navigate is worth more than ten times the prompts with no structure. The [AI Prompt Vault](/product/ai-prompt-vault) is built around this idea: prompts grouped into 12 categories rather than one long list.

## Look for tested prompts, not theoretical ones

A prompt that "should work" and a prompt that's been run and refined against real output are different products. Reviews are your best signal here — check the review count and rating on a listing before buying, and look specifically for reviewers describing an actual use case, not just "great pack!"

## AI avatar and image toolkits

Beyond text prompts, there's a growing category of AI-assisted visual toolkits — avatar generators, style presets, consistent-character templates for creators who need a visual identity without hiring a designer. These work best when they include the actual generation parameters/settings, not just example outputs you can't reproduce.

## Workflow kits: the most underrated category

The most practically useful AI products right now aren't prompt packs at all — they're workflow kits that chain multiple steps together (research → draft → edit → publish) into a repeatable system. These save the most time precisely because they remove decision fatigue, not just typing time.
